title: "Prepaid credits"

The Credit ledgers dashboard reports prepaid wallet activity: credits bought, credits granted, credits spent, credits expired, and what is left on the books.

<Warning>
**Selling credits is not revenue. Spending them is.** A wallet top-up is money received against future consumption. Revenue appears later, when the credits are consumed against an invoice, where it shows as gross revenue with an offsetting contra line. See [Revenue streams](/guide/analytics/revenue/revenue-streams).
</Warning>

## Population

**Included** Settled wallet transactions only.

**Excluded** Pending and failed top-ups. A credit purchase that never settled is not in these reports, even though the customer may have been invoiced for it.

## The four credit types

| Type | Direction | What it is |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Purchased credits | Inflow | The customer bought credits. Money in, revenue later. |
| Offered credits | Inflow | You granted credits, for a trial, a goodwill gesture or a commercial agreement. They cost you revenue when spent. |
| Consumed credits | Outflow | Credits applied to an invoice. |
| Voided credits | Outflow | Credits removed from the wallet, such as expiry or a manual void. |

## Credits metric

Every report on this dashboard has two readings, controlled by the **Credits metric** filter:

- **Amount**, the default: the monetary value of the movement, in the wallet's currency.
- **Credits**: the number of credit units moved.

They are not interchangeable. One credit is worth whatever your wallet's rate says, so a wallet at 1 credit for 2 USD reports 50 credits and 100 USD for the same movement. Say which one you are quoting.

<Warning>
**Breaking credits down by plan or subscription is an allocation.** A wallet transaction belongs to a customer, not to a subscription. When one is tied to an invoice covering several subscriptions, its amount is split evenly across them. Customer-level and currency-level figures are exact. Per-plan and per-subscription figures are approximations.
</Warning>

## Credit ledger movements

Everything that moved in or out of your wallets.

**Included** All four credit types in the period. Inflows positive, consumed and voided credits negative, so the chart reads like a ledger.

**Excluded** Unsettled transactions.

**How it's calculated** Movements summed per period and credit type, with outflows negated.

**Grain and timing** Flow, dated on the settlement date. Adds correctly across periods.

**Currency** The wallet's currency, never converted. Switch to the Credits metric for credit units.

## Purchased credits

Credits your customers bought.

**Included** Settled purchased-credit inflows.

**Excluded** Offered credits, which are a separate line. Consumption and voids.

**How it's calculated** Purchased inflows summed per period.

**Grain and timing** Flow, dated on the settlement date.

**Currency** The wallet's currency, never converted.

## Offered credits

Credits you gave away.

**Included** Settled granted-credit inflows.

**Excluded** Purchased credits. Consumption and voids.

**How it's calculated** Granted inflows summed per period.

**Grain and timing** Flow, dated on the settlement date.

**Currency** The wallet's currency, never converted.

## Credit balances

What your customers still hold.

**Included** The end-of-period wallet balance per customer and currency. Periods with no transactions carry the previous balance forward, so a dormant wallet still shows its balance.

**Excluded** Nothing: it is the running balance of all settled movements.

**How it's calculated** Inflows less outflows, accumulated to the end of each period.

**Grain and timing** **Stock.** One balance per customer, currency and period. Never add balances across periods.

**Currency** The wallet's currency, never converted.

<Note>
Outstanding credit balances are a liability: consumption you owe your customers. They are the mirror image of deferred revenue, and they are the number to watch when credits are sold in bulk and spent slowly.
</Note>

## Reconciliation and common questions

<AccordionGroup>
<Accordion title="A customer bought 10,000 of credits. Why didn't revenue move?">
Because a top-up is not revenue. It appears here as a purchased-credit inflow, and on the [Invoices](/guide/analytics/invoices) dashboard as a credit-type invoice. Revenue appears as the customer consumes the credits against invoices.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Why do prepaid credits show as contra revenue?">
Because the invoice they paid for already reported the full amount as gross revenue. The credits consumed are deducted as a contra line so net revenue is not double counted. Without that line, revenue would be inflated by every credit-funded invoice.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="What is the difference between offered and purchased credits, in accounting terms?">
Purchased credits are cash received against a liability. Offered credits are a discount you have not yet given: they cost you nothing when granted and reduce revenue when consumed, which is why they land in contra revenue as free credits.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Why don't twelve monthly credit balances add up?">
Balances are stocks. December's balance is the year-end balance. To measure the year's activity, use the ledger movements, which are flows.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Credits expired. Where do they show?">
As voided credits, an outflow on the ledger, reducing the balance. Expiry does not create revenue.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Why is a top-up missing?">
It is probably not settled yet. These reports cover settled transactions only. Check the wallet's transaction list in the app. See [Wallets and prepaid credits](/guide/wallet-and-prepaid-credits/overview).
</Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
