---
title: "New subscriptions"
description: "How Lago counts subscription starts, and why a plan change is not a new customer."
---

Subscription starts in the period, dated on the start date and split into two lines that answer different questions.

Four charts: **New subscriptions**, split fresh against plan changes, **Day of new subscriptions**, **New subscriptions by plan**, and **New subscriptions details**, one row per start with the plan the customer came from.

## Fresh subscriptions

Genuinely new subscription relationships.

**Included** Subscription starts with no previous subscription behind them.

**Excluded** Starts that replace an existing subscription, which are plan changes.

**How it's calculated** A count of subscription starts in the period with no predecessor.

**Grain and timing** Flow, dated on the start date.

**Currency** Grouped by the plan's currency.

## Plan changes

Movement inside your existing base.

**Included** Subscription starts linked to a previous subscription: upgrades, downgrades and same-price moves.

**Excluded** First-time starts.

**How it's calculated** A count of subscription starts in the period that carry a predecessor.

**Grain and timing** Flow, dated on the new subscription's start date.

**Currency** Grouped by the plan's currency.

<Note>
A plan change creates a **new subscription record**. It is not a new customer, and the count of fresh subscriptions is the number to quote when someone asks how many customers you added. The details table shows the plan the customer came from.
</Note>

**Day of new subscriptions** plots the same starts by weekday, on a fixed Sunday to Saturday axis. It answers "when do customers sign up", not "how many".

## Reconciliation and common questions

<AccordionGroup>
<Accordion title="How many customers did we actually add?">
Read fresh subscriptions, not the total. The total includes plan changes, which are existing customers moving between plans.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Why does the by-plan chart show more starts than fresh subscriptions?">
Because it counts every start for the plan, including plan changes into it. A customer upgrading into a plan is a start for that plan and not a new customer for you.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="A customer resubscribed after churning. Where does it show?">
As a fresh subscription, since the new subscription has no predecessor. In [MRR](/guide/analytics/revenue/mrr) the same event is expansion rather than new, because the customer is not new to you.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Does a trial count as a start?">
It counts once the subscription starts. Subscriptions with a future start date are not counted until that date arrives.
</Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
