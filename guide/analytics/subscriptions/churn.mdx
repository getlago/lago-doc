---
title: "Churn"
description: "How Lago counts terminations, what it excludes as plan changes, and why subscription churn is not customer churn."
---

Terminations in the period, dated on the termination date and split the same way as starts: real churn on one side, plan changes on the other.

Four charts: **Subscription terminations**, split churn against plan changes, **Day of terminations**, **Churn subscriptions by plan**, which excludes plan changes, and **Churn details**, one row per termination.

## Terminations

Subscriptions that ended and were not replaced.

**Included** Terminated subscriptions with no successor subscription.

**Excluded** Terminations that are the old half of a plan change. Those are counted as plan changes instead.

**How it's calculated** A count of terminated subscriptions in the period with no live successor.

**Grain and timing** Flow, dated on the termination date.

**Currency** Grouped by the plan's currency.

## Plan changes

The old half of an upgrade or a downgrade.

**Included** Terminated subscriptions superseded by a successor that was not itself cancelled.

**Excluded** Terminations with no successor, which are the churn line.

**How it's calculated** A count of terminated subscriptions in the period that have a live successor.

**Grain and timing** Flow, dated on the termination date.

**Currency** Grouped by the plan's currency.

## Churned customers

How many customers the churn represents.

**Included** Distinct customers behind terminations that had no successor, per plan and currency.

**Excluded** Customers whose only terminations were plan changes.

**How it's calculated** A distinct count of customers behind the churn line. The by-plan chart excludes plan changes entirely, so an upgrade never appears as churn.

**Grain and timing** Flow, dated on the termination date.

**Currency** Grouped by the plan's currency.

<Warning>
**Subscription churn is not customer churn.** A customer who terminates one of three subscriptions appears in terminations while remaining a customer. The churn details table carries a flag for exactly this: whether the customer still has another active subscription.
</Warning>

The churn details table adds the context the charts cannot show:

| Column | What it tells you |
| --- | --- |
| Days alive | How long the subscription ran, start to termination. |
| Termination category | Churn, or a plan change classified as an upgrade, a downgrade or a same-price move. Plans on different intervals are compared on their normalized monthly amount, so a move to a cheaper yearly plan reads as a downgrade. |
| Churn type | For churn only: a scheduled end, meaning the subscription reached its planned end date, or a manual termination. |
| Customer still active | For churn only: whether the customer kept another subscription. |
| Next plan | The plan the customer moved to, for plan changes. |

**Day of terminations** plots terminations by weekday, on the same fixed axis as new subscriptions.

## Reconciliation and common questions

<AccordionGroup>
<Accordion title="A customer upgraded. Does that show as churn?">
No. An upgrade adds one to plan changes here and one to plan changes on [New subscriptions](/guide/analytics/subscriptions/new), and nothing to terminations. The by-plan churn chart excludes plan changes altogether.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Does this match MRR churn?">
Not directly. Subscription churn counts subscriptions, MRR churn measures money, and their classifications differ in one case: a termination that leaves the customer with other active subscriptions is a termination here, but contraction rather than churn in [MRR](/guide/analytics/revenue/mrr).
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="How many customers did we actually lose?">
Read churned customers, and check the customer-still-active flag in the details table. A customer who terminated one of several subscriptions is not a lost customer.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="A subscription reached its end date. Is that churn?">
Yes, and the details table labels it a scheduled end rather than a manual termination. Both reduce the base.
</Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
