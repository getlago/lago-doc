## How to read a number

Three kinds of measure live on these dashboards. Mixing them up is the most common source of confusion.

| Kind | What it means | Reports |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Stock** | A balance read at a point in time. Never add it across periods. | Starting and ending MRR, active subscriptions, credit balances |
| **Flow** | An amount or a count that accumulates over the period. Adding it across periods is correct. | MRR movements, revenue streams, usage cost and units, credit ledger movements, new subscriptions, terminations, invoiced amounts, payment attempts, dunning outcome |
| **As-of snapshot** | A picture of today, whatever the date range says. | AR aging, outstanding revenue exposure |

<Warning>
A stock and a flow behave differently when you export. Twelve monthly ending-MRR values do not sum to an annual figure: the December value **is** the year-end balance. Twelve monthly new-MRR values do sum to the year's new MRR.
</Warning>

The active subscriptions reports deserve their own warning: with a date range applied, they answer "how many were active at the end of this range", not "how many were active at some point during it". See [Active subscriptions](/guide/analytics/subscriptions/active).

## Filters

Filters are applied before the numbers are computed, not after they are drawn. A filtered chart is a full recomputation, not a cropped view of a bigger one. Two filters are structural rather than restrictive:

- **Granularity** sets the bucket width: day, week or month. Weeks and months are calendar-based. The current period is shown as far as it has gone, which is why the last bar is usually short.
- **Breakdown** sets which dimensions form the series. Removing a dimension re-aggregates the chart instead of hiding lines.

Each dashboard carries its own filter bar. They do not bleed into each other.

| Dashboard | Filters |
| --- | --- |
| Subscriptions | Date range, Currency, Country, Customers, Customer type, Plan code, Subscription ext. ID, Customer has tax ID |
| Revenue streams and MRR | Date range, Granularity, Breakdown, Currency, Country, Customers, Customer type, Plan code, Subscription ext. ID, Customer has tax ID |
| Usage | Date range, Granularity, Breakdown, Currency, Billable metric, Country, Customers, Customer type, Plan code, Subscription ext. ID, Customer has tax ID |
| Prepaid credits | Date range, Granularity, Credits metric, Currency, Country, Customers, Customer type, Plan code, Subscription ext. ID, Customer has tax ID |
| Cash collection | Date range, Granularity, Invoice number |
| Invoices | Date range, Granularity, Invoice number, Currency, Country, Customers, Customer type, Plan code, Subscription ext. ID, Customer has tax ID |

Subscriptions has no granularity selector: its charts are fixed to a monthly or weekday axis.

## Currency

Amounts are reported in the currency they were billed in. Lago does not convert between currencies and never adds two currencies together. A chart showing several currencies at once stacks them side by side, so the total on screen is not a meaningful number. Filter on one currency, or break the chart down by currency, whenever you need a total you can quote.

Zero-decimal currencies such as JPY and KRW, and three-decimal currencies such as KWD and BHD, are handled at their own precision.

## Excluded everywhere

Two populations are out of scope on the revenue reports, by design:

- **Partner accounts.** Customers flagged as partners are excluded from MRR and revenue streams. Revenue share is reported separately. See [Revenue share](/guide/revenue-share).
- **Self-billed invoices.** Invoices you issue on a partner's behalf never count as your revenue.

## Refresh and caching

The analytical data behind these dashboards is refreshed on a daily cycle, around 12:00 AM UTC, and query results are cached on top of that.

Two consequences:

- A change made today, such as a finalized invoice or a terminated subscription, may not appear until the next refresh.
- The same figure can differ from the transactional views in the app for a few hours after a billing run.

For same-day operational checks, use the invoice and subscription views in the app or the API, not these dashboards.

## Exports

Any chart or table exports to CSV, JSON or Excel. The export honours the filters and breakdown active at the time, so what you download matches what you see.

## API

The same datasets are available through the API, which is what you want for a BI tool or a scheduled reconciliation.

```bash
LAGO_URL="https://api.getlago.com"
API_KEY="__YOUR_API_KEY__"

curl --request GET "$LAGO_URL/api/v1/analytics/mrr?currency=USD" \
  --header "Authorization: Bearer $API_KEY"
```

Usage analytics has its own endpoint and query parameters. See [Retrieve usage](/guide/events/retrieve-usage) and the [usage endpoint reference](/api-reference/customer-usage/usages).

## Composable by design

The dashboards run on [Apache Superset](https://superset.apache.org/). What Lago ships covers the standard finance and usage questions. You can extend it: add a chart, change a breakdown, or build your own dashboard on the same datasets for board reporting or a customer-level deep dive.

## Not the same as revenue recognition

These dashboards report billing activity: what was invoiced, collected, consumed and committed. [Revenue recognition](/guide/revenue-recognition/overview) reports accrual-basis revenue as journal entries, spreading amounts across the periods they were earned in. The two answer different questions and will not match line for line.
